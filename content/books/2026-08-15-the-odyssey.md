I read it because The Odyssey film by Christopher Nolan was coming up, and I wanted to catch up on it. I specifically read Robert Fagles' translation by Penguin, and it was beautiful. It was truly a masterpiece, and what surprised me the most was how cinematic it was. It felt performative, like watching a movie, in fact. Another thing that surprised me was reading through the book. It's a fairly small book of close to 350 pages, but it feels like an eternity once you finish the book. It feels like a lot of things have gone by, and a lot of information, a lot of events, have been condensed into a few pages, and that was interesting. The fact that this was the first written piece of literature made it really special. I had this very special feeling and very special attachment to this book because of that. And lastly, I think the idea of just a man who's just trying to return to his family, to his home, just hits hard. It hits home, and the fact that he's not a perfect man. He's a man with a lot of imperfections and failures. That also is very relatable. Although this is a book that was written 2,500 years ago, set in an alien land with monsters and gods, still it feels very relatable. I feel like that is the power of storytelling.
