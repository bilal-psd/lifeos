Jonathan Swift's 1726 satirical novel, originally titled Travels into Several Remote Nations of the World, recounts the four voyages of ship's surgeon Lemuel Gulliver. In Lilliput, Gulliver towers over a miniature society and views it almost as a god would. In Brobdingnag, a land of giants, he is the small one, put on display as a curiosity. The book satirizes human nature and the travel-writing genre of its time, and it introduced the word Lilliput into popular language.
