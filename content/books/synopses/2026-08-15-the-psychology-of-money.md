Morgan Housel's 2020 book argues that what people do with money depends more on how they behave than on what they know. It runs as a set of short chapters, each built around a story rather than a calculation. Housel works through the biases and blind spots behind ordinary decisions about saving and risk, and makes the case that temperament beats technical skill. The numbers, he argues, rarely explain the choices people make.
